## Product flow

1. The client requests renewal of an explicitly assigned registered domain. The studio offers exact years, resolved privacy and the one-time USD fee through the existing renewal agreement. The client consents directly, then an owner activates the agreement.
2. The owner selects an existing issued, unpaid invoice for that exact client and amount before any payment preparation, receipt, sandbox attempt or other purpose. A permanent signed allocation records the original accepted agreement, request, client and complete invoice identity. The invoice is never released for another purpose when terms end or payments are corrected.
3. The client's request history, workspace and invoice statement show the domain, years, privacy, fee and reserved invoice before payment instructions. Existing verified external-payment recording remains separate. Imported paid balances, unrelated paid invoices, recurring invoices, other hosting/catalog purposes and prior unassigned payment history cannot fund renewal.
4. After the allocated invoice has sufficient verified receipts, the owner reviews current registrar/account/client authority and the exact retained payment evidence. A current password and four explicit confirmations approve one submission. The existing provider gateway and ledger send and retain its result.
5. The saved client/owner view distinguishes provider-reported success, provider-reported failure, a pre-dispatch closure and an unconfirmed outcome. False or an ambiguous result never means no charge occurred. Original requests, offers, receipts and approval history remain available.

A successful provider result permits a separately requested future renewal without changing the original request journal. An unresolved attempted or failed renewal fences the canonical domain across new request IDs, client reassignment and account changes. Declining the request or ending terms does not clear that fence. There is no blind retry or automatic recovery dispatch. Recovery from an unresolved real operation remains an owner investigation; this increment does not invent a provider lookup or waive a retained charge risk.

## Authority, isolation and recovery

The allocation and approval bind exact registered-domain ID/name, current explicit link/revision, original link event/audit, account/revision, raw-key fingerprint and sealed-credential digest. They retain the immutable request/binding/accepted agreement and exact invoice/client/currency/amount/hash. Before DNS, credential resolution and send, the owner and all current evidence are checked again. The common latest registrar intent must agree with its exact paired start-audit order. Failed/unfinished latest history or altered sequence cannot revive older successful authority. No client GET unseals a credential or calls the provider.

Allocation, approval and terminal records have separate retained counts, exact audits, canonical documents and keyed proofs. Readiness checks every signed row and duplicated indexed identity before an indexed absence can authorize invoice reuse or hide a domain attempt. Moving an invoice or domain index and recomputing an unkeyed audit cannot replace the retained keyed original. SQL witnesses supplement, rather than replace, keyed canonical identity validation. Validation streams rows but its work grows with retained history; there is no silent truncation or invented reconstruction of missing authority. Complete simultaneous destruction of every local witness remains outside local recovery guarantees.

The receipt review additionally checks an exact receipt/audit bijection and insertion order after the purpose allocation. Audit IDs and details enter the captured review hash. Global original payment-request and sandbox request/attempt/event/audit correspondence is checked before pristine-invoice admission: orphan sandbox audits lack invoice identity and cannot be assumed unrelated. Existing receipt and recurring-origin implementations remain unchanged.

One shared staff mutex serializes competing fulfillment approvals. Approval and consumed grant commit before network work; no SQL transaction spans DNS or HTTP. The existing durable provider ledger is the only send journal. Callback failures and interrupted results preserve their original reservation and cannot authorize another send. An operation-specific credential-owned guard rejects raw/exact Base64 credential echoes before boolean classification. Existing generic response handling remains unchanged for other operations.

Ordinary invoices and existing request history remain usable before the optional renewal/payment/provider families are prepared. Established missing or damaged history fails closed. Malformed or unmatched captured selectors produce an explicit reload/review message and cannot receive silently refreshed authority in the retry form. Support identities cannot approve or access private renewal-payment authority; direct-client ownership is checked separately from owner financial permissions.
