# Private registration drafts

This increment saves a proposed domain and private registrant contact for studio review. It does not perform a provider lookup, register a name, reserve availability, quote a fee, create an agreement or invoice, record payment, or record registry agreement consent. The provider's registration term field and response contract remain unresolved; see the [verified registration contract](TWENTYI_DOMAIN_REGISTRATION_CONTRACT.md).

## Client and owner workflow

An active client signed in directly can save a complete draft, submit the current revision for studio review, withdraw it, or explicitly reopen it. Owners can prepare storage and create or revise drafts for active clients with their current password. Owners cannot submit on the client's behalf. Editing a submitted draft returns it to draft status. Editing a withdrawn draft keeps it withdrawn until the client reopens it. Support sessions and delegated administrators cannot view or edit these private drafts.

The saved scope includes a canonical ASCII domain, one to ten **requested** years, an explicit requested privacy preference, the contact fields established by the retained reference, and one to thirteen explicit nameservers. These are local planning inputs, not a declaration of registry eligibility or the provider's registration wire format. In this first workflow, contact extensions remain empty and registry acceptance remains null, including `.ca` drafts. Drafts must be complete before saving. There is no incomplete autosave, contact import, cross-client reassignment, deletion, provider sender, or payment flow.

Each client can retain up to 100 drafts. Each draft can retain up to 256 revisions. Lists fetch 20 records plus one lookahead; the next-page link appears only when another record exists. The owner's new-draft selector pages through active clients in groups of 100 with one lookahead, so later clients remain accessible through normal navigation. The editor shows the current contact. Earlier revision summaries identify the action, author role and recorded time; their complete encrypted documents remain retained for recovery without exposing old contacts in lists.

## Authority and immutable history

All reads and mutations use the application's existing account mutex and transaction. The current owner or direct-client identity is checked before history validation or decryption. Mutations require the exact captured draft, revision, head digest and unique command. Owner edits also require the exact current target client's account version. Client account changes invalidate captured owner forms and old sessions. Exact command replay requires current authority and all original command fields; it adds no row or audit. A failed form retains its captured fields. Malformed or unmatched selectors do not receive a fresh review token.

Two dedicated InnoDB tables hold mutable current heads and append-only encrypted revisions. Before any indexed lookup, readiness checks the exact schema, independently signed global count and tail, original preparation audit, every canonical revision and keyed proof, all duplicated index fields, every ciphertext digest, exact revision/audit identity, each draft's complete previous-hash and monotonic-time chain, and current head correspondence. Keyset scans start without a lower-bound filter so empty or space-trimmed corrupted identifiers cannot escape validation; every page is bounded to 64 rows and visited counts must match the signed witness. A current request cannot fall back to an older intact row after later history is lost or damaged.

There is no read repair, implicit migration, counter reset or replacement authority. The threat model covers partial or altered retained history while original keyed evidence survives. It does not claim to detect a coherent rollback of the entire database and all of its original signed witnesses to one earlier backup, or compromise of the application signing key.

## Private key and recovery

Complete contact documents are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a fresh 12-byte nonce and 16-byte tag. Associated data binds the installation prefix, draft, client, revision and command. Public envelopes contain only the bounded draft selection, actor/account identity, ciphertext digest and a purpose-separated keyed content equality witness. Raw contact details and unkeyed plaintext digests are excluded from public heads, audit detail and settings.

The separate 32-byte `registration-master.key` lives in existing private storage outside the application and document root. It must be a regular, non-symlink file with exact owner-only `0600` permissions. Initial creation is limited to explicit authorized preparation with no surviving draft history. The original key fingerprint is retained in the signed checkpoint and original setup audit. A missing or replaced established key refuses access and setup; it is never regenerated to make existing drafts appear empty. Back up the original key, application signing key and database together, and restore their exact original context together. The native Windows `0600` guard remains intact.

Only an authorized target draft is decrypted after the global authenticated history checks. Client and owner pages send `Cache-Control: no-store, private` and `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`. Ordinary object JSON/debug projections of the reused registration document omit the private contact; explicit authorized editor access is required to obtain the private fields. Synthetic fixture reports retain hashes and finite metadata, not private keys or contact dumps.
